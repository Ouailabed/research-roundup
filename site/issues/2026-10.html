Global Spillovers from U.S. Bond Supply Shocks

Danila Smirnov
IMF, 24 October 2026

The paper investigates how unexpected changes in U.S. Treasury supply affect global financial conditions and real activity. Using identified bond-supply shocks around auction announcements as instruments, the author estimates daily and monthly local projections for both advanced and emerging economies. Results show that a debt expansion shock that raises the U.S. 10-year yield by 10 bps lifts foreign 10-year yields by roughly 7 bps within a few business days. U.S. industrial production falls by up to 0.4 percent after eight months and foreign industrial production by a comparable amount, with a deeper decline where foreign investor participation is higher.

Key takeaways

  • A shock that raises the U.S. 10-year yield by 10 bps lifts foreign 10-year yields by roughly 7 bps within a few business days.
  • U.S. industrial production falls by up to 0.4 percent after eight months, and foreign industrial production by a comparable amount with a more persistent profile.
  • Effects are stronger with higher foreign investor participation and weaker domestic fundamentals

Why it matters: Understanding how U.S. bond supply moves influence global yields, exchange rates, and growth helps the research team forecast interest-rate environments, currency dynamics, and credit conditions across markets.

Tagged Transversal: The abstract examines spillovers to both advanced economies and emerging markets.

Do Markets Dream of Artificial Intelligence? Macroeconomic Effects and International Spillovers of AI News Shocks

Maria Sole Pagliari
De Nederlandsche Bank, October 2026

The paper asks how AI-related news influences macroeconomic activity and spillovers across countries. It identifies AI news shocks using a daily equity factor and Bayesian VAR with narrative restrictions, then estimates effects with monthly local projections. Findings show AI news triggers an investment-led boost in US capital-goods orders, early rises in producer prices, and in the euro area higher equity prices, euro depreciation and inflation pressures before real activity picks up. The authors highlight a monetary-policy dilemma as price pressures arrive ahead of broader productivity gains.

Key takeaways

  • AI news shocks generate an early surge in capital-goods orders and investment in the United States.
  • In the US, producer prices increase before consumer prices.
  • The results highlight a monetary policy dilemma: financial spillovers and imported price pressures arrive before the broader productive gains from AI adoption.

Why it matters: Understanding AI-driven news shocks helps forecast inflation dynamics, interest-rate moves, currency shifts and sector-specific investment trends, all of which are critical for asset-allocation, risk-management and rate-policy analysis.

Tagged DM: The abstract studies the United States and the euro area, both advanced economies.

Transmission of U.S. Long-term Rate to EM Local Currency Sovereign Bonds

Zixuan Huang, Xiang-Li Lim
IMF, 3 October 2026

The paper examines how shocks to the U.S. long-term risk-neutral interest rate affect emerging-market local-currency sovereign bond yields and term premia. It uses an instrumental-variable approach with data on investor types in these bond markets. The authors find that U.S. rate increases raise EM sovereign yields, but higher domestic investor participation, especially banks and non-bank institutions, mitigates the spillover. The study highlights the role of broadening local investor bases to enhance market resilience.

Key takeaways

  • U.S. long-term rate hikes raise EM local-currency sovereign yields and term premia.
  • Domestic investor participation dampens these spillovers.
  • Broadening the domestic investor base can improve resilience to external rate shocks.

Why it matters: Understanding how U.S. rate movements transmit to EM sovereign yields helps the markets team assess credit risk, price sovereign exposure, and shape strategies for emerging-market portfolios.

Tagged EM: The paper studies shocks to U.S. rates on emerging-market local-currency sovereign bonds, an emerging-economy focus.

Monetary-Policy Risk and Equilibrium Asset Prices

Corey Feldman, Thomas B. King
Chicago Fed, October 2026

The paper asks how uncertainty about monetary policy affects asset prices. It examines day-to-day movements in nominal and real yields, stock returns and implied volatilities around FOMC communications and blackout days, and builds a New Keynesian model with stochastic policy-rule variation. The model shows that monetary-policy risk contributes to excess returns on equities and nominal bonds and accounts for much of the risk premium on inflation-indexed bonds. The effective lower bound reduces the size of this risk but magnifies its asset-pricing consequences.

Key takeaways

  • Yields, stock returns and implied volatilities vary more on days with FOMC communication, consistent with policy communication being a source of risk.
  • In the model, monetary-policy risk contributes to excess returns on equities and nominal bonds and accounts for much of the risk premium on inflation-indexed bonds.
  • The effective lower bound reduces the size of monetary-policy risk but magnifies its asset-pricing consequences.

Why it matters: Understanding how monetary-policy uncertainty is priced helps forecast movements in Treasury yields, equity markets and inflation-linked securities, informs risk-premia models, and guides portfolio allocation and hedging strategies around FOMC events.

Tagged DM: The paper studies monetary-policy risk around FOMC announcements in the United States, an advanced economy.

The Inflation Attention Threshold and Inflation Surges

Oliver Pfäuti
American Economic Review, 30 October 2026

The paper asks when and how public attention to inflation shifts and what macro consequences follow. Using empirical estimation, it finds an attention threshold at 4% inflation, with attention doubling beyond that point. A structural model shows that higher attention amplifies and prolongs the impact of supply shocks on inflation, helping to explain recent inflation spikes, the link with expectations, and the slow pace of disinflation.

Key takeaways

  • Attention to inflation doubles when inflation exceeds an estimated threshold of 4 percent.
  • During high-attention periods, supply shocks generate stronger and more persistent inflationary effects.
  • The attention mechanism jointly explains inflation surges, the co-movement with expectations, and a prolonged disinflation process.

Why it matters: Understanding how inflation attention shifts can improve forecasts of price dynamics and expectations, which are crucial for bond market pricing, central-bank policy assessment, and inflation-linked investment strategies.

Tagged Transversal: The abstract presents a theoretical and empirical analysis of inflation attention without mentioning any specific country or region.

A New Model of Trend Inflation Using Disaggregates, Survey Expectations, and Uncertainty

Ellis W. Tallman, Saeed Zaman
Cleveland Fed, 31 October 2026

The paper asks how to improve estimates of trend inflation by adding recent advances such as survey-based long-term expectations, sectoral disaggregation, stochastic volatility, a time-varying Phillips curve, and uncertainty effects. It uses a Bayesian estimation framework to fit the model to aggregate and sectoral inflation data. The results show that the new specification gives a more interpretable decomposition of inflation history, and that uncertainty effects play a greater role than business cycle effects in explaining inflation fluctuations.

Key takeaways

  • The model combines survey-based long-term inflation expectations with sectoral data, and gives a more interpretable historical decomposition than the models it extends.
  • The model allows for stochastic volatility and for time-varying uncertainty effects on the level of inflation.
  • Uncertainty effects matter more than business-cycle effects for explaining inflation fluctuations.

Why it matters: Better trend-inflation models help the research team forecast price dynamics, assess inflation risks, and support central-bank and market expectations for interest-rate policy.

Tagged Transversal: The abstract develops a general model of trend inflation without naming any country or region.

Transformative AI and demand bottlenecks

Phurichai Rungcharoenkitkul
BIS, 12 October 2026

The paper asks how transformative AI, by raising the share of investment in output, could constrain consumer demand and thereby limit further AI-driven growth. It builds an endogenous growth model that incorporates research and entry decisions and calibrates it to empirical evidence. The authors find that a demand bottleneck is a highly plausible scenario, and the model projects more moderate growth in the coming years than recent studies. Policy implications, including for the natural rate of interest, are discussed.

Key takeaways

  • AI-driven productivity gains may be offset by a demand bottleneck that slows overall growth
  • Calibrated simulations suggest moderate growth prospects rather than the high rates projected by other studies
  • The paper draws policy implications, including for the natural rate of interest.

Why it matters: Understanding whether AI will fuel strong growth or encounter a demand bottleneck helps forecast future output, investment returns, and the trajectory of interest rates, all of which are crucial for asset allocation, rate positioning, and risk assessment by market participants.

Tagged Transversal: The abstract develops a global endogenous growth model without referencing any specific country or region.

The Causal Effects of Global Supply Chain Disruptions on Macroeconomic Outcomes: Evidence and Theory

Xiwen Bai, Jesús Fernández‐Villaverde, Yiliang Li, Francesco Zanetti
American Economic Review, 30 October 2026

The authors build a real-time index of port congestion from AIS data on container ships and use a spatial clustering algorithm to capture supply chain disruptions. They introduce a search-friction model that links upstream production slack to downstream shortages, allowing identification of output and price responses. Using this framework they show that demand and supply shocks drove US disinflation in 2020, while the 2021 inflation surge was mainly due to supply-chain shocks.

Key takeaways

  • A real-time port congestion index is constructed using AIS data and spatial clustering.
  • A search-friction model links upstream production slack to downstream shortages, identifying output and price responses.
  • Demand and supply shocks caused US disinflation in 2020, while the 2021 inflation surge was driven mainly by supply-chain shocks.

Why it matters: Understanding whether recent inflation spikes are driven by supply-chain disruptions or demand factors helps the markets team forecast CPI trends, assess monetary policy outlook, and price related assets such as commodities and inflation-linked securities.

Tagged DM: The paper studies US supply chain shocks, an advanced economy.
